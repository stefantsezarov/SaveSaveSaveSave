Spot the Trap · Challenge 1 of 4

The job that asks for a deposit

An unexpected chat message offers online work. You decide to hear more. Later, after completing a set of tasks, the recruiter sends another message.

Invented exchange · Chat

Recruiter

We have remote work rating product images.1 Around 20 minutes a day. You could earn $120–$300 daily.2

Recruiter

Your tasks are complete. Your balance is now $486.3

Recruiter

Before you can withdraw, deposit 49 USDT to activate Level 24. You'll get the deposit back with your payout. Please finish today.5

What would you do next?

The explanation

Each option

  • A: The balance on the screen is a claim. Sending a deposit does not establish that those earnings exist or can be withdrawn.
  • B: Asking questions is sensible, but a document supplied by the same person does not independently verify the offer.
  • C — recommended: Pause the payment. If you investigate further, use a contact route you find independently.
  • D: A smaller payment is still money at risk. It does not verify the offer.

Details to notice

  1. An unexpected job approach needs checking.
  2. The offer promises unusually large rewards for brief, repetitive work.
  3. The displayed balance is not proof of payment.
  4. A deposit is required to release the supposed earnings.
  5. The deadline discourages time to check.

Remember: Do not send a deposit to unlock supposed wages or earnings.

About this pattern

The FTC describes task scams involving repetitive online work, displayed earnings and demands for deposits, often in cryptocurrency. FTC: Task scams create the illusion of making money

The source documents the technique, not this invented example.
