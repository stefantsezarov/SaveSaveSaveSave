Spot the Trap · Challenge 2 of 4

The call about a “safe account”

A caller says they work for your bank. Your phone displays the bank's name. During the call, this message arrives.

Bank security4

We have detected suspicious access to your account. Move your savings to the secure holding account discussed on the phone.1 Use reference SAFE-4471. Do this today.3 Do not speak to branch staff while the investigation is ongoing.2

What would you do next?

Your answer

Next time: A request to move money into a “safe account” is a reason to stop and contact your bank independently.

Each option explained
  • A — recommended: Break contact and check through the bank's established channel. Do not transfer money on this caller's instructions.
  • B: Knowing personal information does not establish that someone represents your bank.
  • C: Two messages from the same source do not provide independent confirmation.
Details to notice
  1. The caller directs a transfer to supposedly protect your savings.
  2. The request discourages contact with other bank staff.
  3. The deadline adds pressure.
  4. A familiar-looking sender name does not establish who controls the conversation.
About this pattern

The FTC describes fake fraud alerts that lead to callers pressuring people to move money to supposedly protect it. FTC: Text scams that may target your business

The source documents the technique, not this invented example.
