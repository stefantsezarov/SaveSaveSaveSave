2. What the service does

SaveSaveSaveSave screens two kinds of thing and reports what it found:

  • Crypto addresses and token contracts, checked against third-party security data across the supported chains.
  • Pasted text — a message, post or AI prompt — checked in your browser against a fixed set of detection rules.

It reports evidence, names its sources, and states what it could not check.

3. What the verdicts mean

4. Limits you should assume

  • Detection in the message scanner is pattern-based. There is no meaning-level analysis, so a technique phrased in an unusual way can pass unnoticed.
  • Links are examined for structure only. No address in your text is visited and no reputation service is consulted.
  • Address data comes from third-party providers and public blockchain nodes. It can be incomplete, out of date, or wrong.
  • Coverage differs by chain and by scan type, and the interface says which supports what.
  • Blockchain addresses are pseudonymous. Interaction with a flagged address is not proof of wrongdoing by anyone.
  • A prompt that looks harmless can still be dangerous once an AI system has your files, your browser, a wallet, or the ability to act.
