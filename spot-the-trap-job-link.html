Spot the Trap · Challenge 4 of 4

The job offer with a link to sign in

You have been applying for jobs online. An email arrives from a recruiter you do not recognise.

Talent team

Congratulations! You have been selected for a remote assistant role, no interview needed.1 To accept, sign in at hr-onboarding.example/start with your email address and email password.2 Then download and open the attached offer file.3 Add your bank card details so we can set up your first salary payment.4 This offer expires in 24 hours.5

What would you do next?

Your answer

Next time: Do not click links, open attachments or share passwords or card details from a job offer until you have checked the employer yourself.

Each option explained
  • A: A sign-in page reached from an unexpected message can be a copy built to capture your password. A job offer does not make the link safe.
  • B: An attachment from a sender you have not verified can install malware. Opening it gives the sender what they want before you know who they are.
  • C: The sender cannot confirm their own link. Any reassurance comes from the same unverified source.
  • D — recommended: Check through a route you find yourself. A real employer will still have the role, or a way to reach the recruiter, there.
Details to notice
  1. A job offered without an interview or any checking of you needs checking itself.
  2. The link asks for your email password, which no employer needs.
  3. An unexpected attachment can carry malware.
  4. Card details are requested before you have verified the employer.
  5. The short deadline discourages time to check.
About this pattern

The FTC explains how phishing messages use links and attachments to steal passwords and account details, and how to check a message through a source you trust. FTC: How to recognize and avoid phishing scams

The source documents the technique, not this invented example.
